# Building-block selection

Start with an approved objective and cited source. Choose the smallest activity that creates the
learner action the objective requires.

## Selection map

- Explain/describe: rich text, callout, steps, example, comparison or timeline.
- Recall/identify: flip cards, short answer, blanks, true/false or multiple choice.
- Compare/distinguish: contrasting examples, comparison table, sorting or labelled figure.
- Sequence/perform: steps, ordering, worked example, then scaffolded practice.
- Decide/evaluate: case, branching scenario or choice with consequences and explanatory feedback.
- Locate/inspect: labelled figure or hotspots plus an equivalent ordered text list.
- Apply/solve: practice activity, GIFT quiz, LiaScript self-check, code exercise or project.
- Explore/model: curated interactive; use a generated simulation only when the sandbox feature,
  quality loop and explicit author approval exist.
- Reflect/create: open response, Cornell notes or project; do not pretend it can be objectively
  auto-graded.

## Required practice structure

1. Activate relevant prior knowledge.
2. Give a toolbox from cited definitions, formulas or references.
3. Increase challenge: guided → supported → independent.
4. Reveal hints in tiers: nudge → pointer → near-solution.
5. Explain why an answer works or fails.
6. Show a worked solution only after an attempt.

## H5P rule

The policy lists all official H5P types. Only `authoring: api-generated` may be generated by the
builder. `author-import` means the author supplies/edits a package. Never synthesize raw H5P params
for an unimplemented mapper.

Prefer native GIFT questions where both formats teach the same thing: they are easier to cite,
diff, track and update. Choose H5P when its interaction materially adds value.

## Avoid

- decorative interaction unrelated to an objective;
- drag-only tasks;
- timed tasks when speed is not the objective;
- flash cards for conceptual application;
- multiple choice when a learner needs to produce or perform;
- visual-only diagrams without text;
- external embeds without origin, privacy and accessibility review;
- interaction before explanation and guidance;
- repeating the same interaction pattern until it becomes mechanical.
