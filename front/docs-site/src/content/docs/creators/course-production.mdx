Open a course you built with the Course Builder and choose **Production** in the studio navigation
(`/studio/courses/<id>/production`). The page answers three questions about that one course, in English, Polish or
Chinese (the language links at the top).

- **How long did it take?** Every stage the Course Builder ran (the outline, the lessons, the grounding check,
  interactive elements, quizzes, metadata, the apply step, element edits by chat and updates from sources) with its
  time. The totals separate **generation time** (what the machine did while you waited) from **interview time** (you
  answering questions) and **model time** (the summed time of the model calls, which is longer because lessons are
  written in parallel). A stage says how it was measured: start to finish, whole run only (builds older than 30 days),
  or did not finish normally.
- **What did it cost?** The model calls that made and changed the course (build cost, including variants) and those
  that kept it up to date (maintenance cost: proposals from sources and from learner insights), with the number of
  calls and how many failed. Failed calls are included because they were paid for. Tutoring and personal help for
  learners are not part of producing a course and are not counted.
- **What became of what the AI wrote?** The modules, lessons, blocks and quiz questions the AI wrote, split into those
  you kept as written, those the AI revised on your request, those an author edited and those from before this was
  recorded (shown as unknown, never guessed). Elements an author wrote from scratch are listed apart. The split is a bar
  with the same numbers in a table.

**Author time** shows "Not recorded" unless your administrator turns review time on; it is never shown as zero.
**Download CSV** and **Download JSON** save the figures of this course with the same columns as the administrator's
export (see [Authoring metrics](/operators/authoring-metrics/)).

These are your academy's own measurements. The page does not compare the course with other courses, other academies or
any industry figure. The page is read by the author of the course and by administrators; another author's course answers
that it belongs to someone else.
