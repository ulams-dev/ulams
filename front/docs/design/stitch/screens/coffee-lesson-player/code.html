Chapter IV · Science of Solubles · Folio Reference 04.1

Ratios & extraction: The Mathematical Geometry of Solubles

person_edit Tomasz Wierzba, Agronomist · schedule 18 min directed study · SCA Golden Cup Standard
Listen to narrated monograph Voice: Tomasz Wierzba · Full master audio
02:01 / 08:24

In specialty coffee agronomy and sensory science, water is not a passive carrier but a relentless solvent. When water meets roasted coffee cells, it selectively dissolves chemical compounds in a strictly ordered chronological sequence: fruit acids and mineral salts rush forth within the opening thirty seconds, followed by sweet aromatic lipids and cellulose sugars, and finally—if contact prolongs unchecked—the astringent phenols and heavy wood chlorogenic acids that ruin balance.

Understanding the physics of infusion requires reconciling two fundamental metrics: Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), which denotes beverage strength or concentration, and Extraction Yield (EY%), which computes the precise proportion of dry bean mass surrendered to the cup. Without both coordinates, brewing remains an unpredictable gamble rather than a repeatable scientific ritual.

The Extraction Yield Equation SCA Technical Monograph IV
EY% = TDS% × Beverage Mass (g) / Dry Coffee Dose (g)
TDS% Refractometer optical index measured strictly at 20°C calibrated with distilled baseline.
Beverage Mass Total liquid yield accumulated on precision scale (±0.1g) in the decanting carafe.
Dry Coffee Dose Initial dry ground grist weighed prior to pre-infusion bloom cycle (±0.05g).
Target Golden Cup Window: 18.0% – 22.0% EY Ideal TDS range: 1.25% – 1.45%

When we brew with high-elevation Geisha or washed Ethiopian Heirloom varietals grown at 2,100 meters, cellular density demands elevated thermal kinetic energy. The pore matrix of dense seeds releases its soluble content with reluctance. A brew that finishes at only 15.5% extraction yield leaves delicate malic acids unbalanced by sweetness, perceived by the palate as aggressive, salty lime peel.

Plate IV.A: Optical Brix refractometer calibration against a 1:16.6 extraction matrix. Terroir Research Station, Boquete

Extraction Yield vs. Sensory Profile

Yield Window Classification Tasting Profile Remedial Action
14.0% – 17.5% Under-extracted Sour, unbalanced sharpness, thin mouthfeel, pronounced vegetal saltiness. Finer grind, elevate brew temp to 94°C, extend contact time.
18.0% – 22.0% Optimal (Golden Cup) Sweet panela finish, ripe stone fruit acidity, round body, lingering floral resonance. Maintain parameters; log grind micron size.
22.5% – 28.0% Over-extracted Astringent, mouth-drying bitterness, hollow center, persistent burnt woody tannin. Coarsen grind setting, reduce agitation pours, lower water to 90°C.
