This course reads Poland through public statistics: an interactive map behind the lesson, charts you can open, and a source for every number. It is not an argument for or against anyone. It records what the institutions that publish the data say, in the year they say it.

## How a lesson works

1. **Explore the map.** The map stays behind the text. Use Back and Next, or the step list, to move through the chapter. Each step shows the figures it is about in a panel.
2. **Read the explanation.** A short text names what you are looking at and how to read it.
3. **Practise.** A card set, a table, a timeline or a practice activity, then a short quiz. The quiz tells you straight away what you got right.

## How the numbers are treated

- **Every figure names its publisher.** The statistics office, the regulator, the ministry or the company that published it. The Sources section of each lesson lists the pages.
- **Every figure has a period.** "In 2025" and "June 2026" are not the same, and the data will date. When you read this course later, check the publisher for newer numbers.
- **Estimates are called estimates.** A forecast or a survey result is labelled as one.
- **Not everything is good news.** The last chapter puts what is still hard next to what is moving.

There is no chapter on education: the data behind the map has none, and adding it would need new research.
