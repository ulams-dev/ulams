::: richtext title="Reading the prosperity numbers" duration="12 min"
**PPS is output adjusted for prices.** GDP per person in purchasing power standards (PPS) corrects for the fact that a euro buys different amounts in different countries. In 2025 Poland stood at 81% of the EU average, level with Portugal and ahead of Hungary and Greece {{src:PL-002}}. It is not a measure of wages, and not a complete ranking of living standards.

**Nominal and per person differ.** The IMF puts Polish GDP in 2025 at $1.036 trillion in current US dollars. Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Belarus, Ukraine, Moldova, Romania and Bulgaria together come to $1.076 trillion, but with 75.4 million people against Poland's 36.5 million {{src:IMF25}}. Per person that is roughly $28.4k against $14.3k, our calculation from the IMF's GDP and population. Exchange rates can move this comparison from year to year.

**Growth is a rate, not a level.** In the second quarter of 2026 GDP was 3.7% higher than a year earlier (Eurostat's flash estimate of 14 August), against 1.2% in the EU and 2.1% in the United States. Statistics Poland's later preliminary estimate is 3.8% {{src:PL-136}}. In 2025 average disposable income per person rose 6.7% after inflation {{src:PL-004}}.

**Prices matter.** Real household consumption in Poland was 31.8% higher in 2025 than in 2016, against 10.4% in the euro area and 10.1% in Germany {{src:PL-141}}. Consumer prices are 73% of the EU average {{src:PL-009}}.

**Poverty is relative.** 15.0% of people in Poland were at risk of poverty or social exclusion in 2025, against 20.9% in the EU; only Czechia was lower {{src:PL-007}}. That is a relative EU measure, not an absolute poverty line. Average gross pay in the enterprise sector was PLN 9,401.58 in June 2026 {{src:PL-146}}.
:::
