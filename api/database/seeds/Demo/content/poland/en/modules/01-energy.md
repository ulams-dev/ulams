Two numbers open the energy chapter. On 29 September 2026 gas storage was 99% full, about 3.3 billion cubic metres {{src:PL-111}}. At the end of 2025 Poland counted 1,636,673 renewable micro-installations, about 13.9 GW, mostly rooftop solar {{src:PL-115}}.

::: richtext title="Reading the energy numbers" duration="10 min"
Four ideas help with this chapter.

**Storage is a share of capacity.** Gas Storage Poland reported its facilities 99% full on 29 September 2026, about 3.3 billion cubic metres of working gas {{src:PL-111}}. That says how full the tanks are, not how much a winter will need, and a full store does not remove high prices. Supply comes through Baltic Pipe from Norway (up to 10 bcm a year since October 2022) {{src:PL-114}} and the Świnoujście LNG terminal, expanded to 8.3 bcm a year in January 2025 {{src:PL-113}}.

**Capacity is not output.** A solar panel rated for a given power produces that only in good light. The regulator counts 1,636,673 renewable micro-installations at the end of 2025 with about 13.9 GW of capacity, and they fed 8.95 TWh into the grid in 2025 {{src:PL-115}}.

**Shares move for two reasons.** The coal share of electricity fell from 79.7% to 61.3% between 2021 and 2025. That is a fall of 18.4 percentage points. It is a share of what the national power system generated, our calculation from the grid operator's annual tables {{src:PL-176}}; other sources use other bases and give other percentages. Over the same years wind and other renewables more than doubled, from 19.0 to 42.7 TWh {{src:PL-174}}. Poland's first offshore wind farm, Baltic Power, sent its first electricity to the grid on 10 July 2026 {{src:PL-122}}.

**Food is energy of another kind.** In 2025 Polish agri-food exports reached €58.4 billion with a €19.8 billion surplus, so imports were about €38.6 billion. About a quarter of export value went outside the EU {{src:PL-123}}.
:::
