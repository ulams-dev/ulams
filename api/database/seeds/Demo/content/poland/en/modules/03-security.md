::: richtext title="Reading the security numbers" duration="10 min"
**Spending as a share of GDP.** NATO's table of 2026 estimates puts Polish defence spending at 4.68% of GDP (an estimated 4.25% in 2025), against 3.17% for the United States and 2.53% for European allies and Canada together. In the same table the three Baltic states report higher shares {{src:PL-178}}. These are estimates with a cut-off of 3 July 2026, not outturns, and spending is not the same as readiness.

**Deterrence lowers a risk.** NATO now reports nine battlegroups along its eastern flank, from Finland to Bulgaria, and two operations launched in 2025: Eastern Sentry, which keeps vigilance along the eastern flank, and Baltic Sentry, which protects critical undersea infrastructure in the Baltic {{src:PL-126}}. Collective deterrence lowers the risk; it does not remove it.

**Projects have stages.** ORP Wicher was launched in August 2026 in Gdynia, a 138.7-metre frigate and the first of three Miecznik-class ships. A launched ship is not yet a ship in service: it is planned to enter service in 2029 and the programme is due to finish in 2032 {{src:PL-185}}. Official sources date the ceremony 12 and 13 August, so this course says only August {{src:PL-184}}. ICEYE agreed in May 2025 to supply satellites to the Polish Armed Forces {{src:PL-081}}.

**Perception is not an assessment.** In an April 2026 survey by CBOS 97% described their own neighbourhood or village as safe and peaceful and 89% said people live safely in Poland; the share afraid of becoming a crime victim fell five points, to 36% {{src:PL-108}}. The Global Peace Index 2026 ranks Poland 22nd, up 23 places from 45th a year earlier {{src:PL-172}}. The first is perceived safety, the second a composite index; neither is a military assessment.
:::
