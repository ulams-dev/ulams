::: interactive title="Bad, and better, at the same time" start=c-ledger end=ledger duration="8 min"
Things can be hard and getting better at the same time. The last step puts three problems next to three improvements: demography, public money and energy. None cancels the other {{src:PL-193}}.

**Try this.** Read each row of the ledger twice, left then right. Is any number on the left contradicted by a number on the right? Why not?
:::

::: richtext title="Reading the ledger" duration="8 min"
**Still hard.** The total fertility rate fell from 1.16 in 2023 to 1.10 in 2024 {{src:PL-193}}. The 2027 draft budget assumes a general government deficit of 7.1% of GDP {{src:PL-192}}. Coal still produced 61.3% of electricity in 2025 {{src:PL-176}}.

**Moving.** Men live 8.7 years and women 7 years longer than in 1990 {{src:PL-099}}, and 78.8% of 20 to 64 year olds work {{src:PL-010}}. GDP grew 3.7% in the second quarter of 2026, against 1.2% in the EU {{src:PL-136}}. Gas storage was 99% full, and wind and other renewables more than doubled since 2021 {{src:PL-111}} {{src:PL-174}}.

**Two lists, no verdict.** The point of the ledger is that a problem and an improvement can both be true. A figure on one side does not cancel a figure on the other. Whether a deficit is a problem, or how fast coal should fall, is a choice about priorities that the numbers inform but do not decide.

**What to do with this course.** Check the publisher for newer numbers, name the period when you quote one, and say which kind of number it is: a count, a rate, an estimate, a survey answer or a plan.
:::
