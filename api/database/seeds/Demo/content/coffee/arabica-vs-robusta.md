# Arabica vs Robusta

*Two species, one drink, and a long argument about which of them deserves your morning.*

Almost every cup of coffee you have ever drunk came from one of two plants: **_Coffea arabica_** or **_Coffea canephora_**, the species the trade calls **Robusta**. They look alike on the branch. Their red cherries are the same size, their white flowers smell of jasmine for the same two or three days a year. But put them side by side in a cupping bowl and they could hardly be more different.

> "Arabica is the coffee you taste. Robusta is the coffee you feel."
> — Inés Duarte

## A plant from the Ethiopian forest

Arabica evolved in the cloud forests of south-western Ethiopia and the Boma Plateau of South Sudan, as a natural cross between two older species, *C. canephora* and *C. eugenioides*. That accident gave it four sets of chromosomes instead of two, so Arabica pollinates itself. It is great for farmers, who get a plant that breeds true. It is bad for resilience: most Arabica in the world descends from a few seeds carried out of Yemen in the 17th and 18th centuries, so the crop has very little genetic diversity.

Robusta grows wild across the lowland forests of West and Central Africa, from Guinea to Uganda. It needs cross-pollination, so every tree is genetically different. That diversity is why Robusta resists coffee leaf rust and heat far better than Arabica.

## Side by side

| | Arabica | Robusta |
|---|---|---|
| Share of world production | ~56 % | ~44 % |
| Typical altitude | 1,000–2,400 m | 0–900 m |
| Ideal temperature | 15–24 °C | 24–30 °C |
| Caffeine (dry weight) | 1.2–1.5 % | 2.2–2.7 % |
| Sugars (sucrose) | 6–9 % | 3–7 % |
| Lipids | 15–17 % | 10–11.5 % |
| Chlorogenic acids | 5.5–8 % | 7–10 % |
| Bean shape | Oval, curved centre cut | Rounder, straight centre cut |
| Cup | Sweet, aromatic, bright acidity | Heavy body, woody, bitter-chocolate |
| Main producers | Brazil, Colombia, Ethiopia, Honduras | Vietnam, Brazil (conilon), Indonesia, Uganda |

The extra caffeine and chlorogenic acids in Robusta are not an accident: both are bitter, and both deter insects. A plant growing in the hot lowlands, where pests thrive, needs more chemical protection. Arabica, safe in cool mountain air, spends that energy on sugars and aromatic oils instead.

> **Why altitude matters**
>
> Every 100 m of altitude lowers the average temperature by about 0.6 °C. On a farm at 1,750 m in Huila, nights drop to 12–14 °C. The cooler the night, the slower the cherry ripens. A slow-ripening cherry has more time to build sugars and organic acids in the seed, and a denser bean. Dense beans roast more evenly and taste more complex: more citric and malic acidity, more sweetness, more aroma. The same Caturra plant grown at 900 m will give a flatter, heavier cup. This is why coffee labels print the altitude: it is the closest thing to a quality promise that fits on a bag.

## Is Robusta "bad" coffee?

For most of the 20th century, Robusta was the cheap filler of instant coffee and supermarket blends, often harvested unripe and dried carelessly. The species got the blame for the processing. Today a growing group of producers in Uganda, India and Vietnam pick only ripe cherries and process them with the same care as specialty Arabica. These **fine Robustas** can taste of dark chocolate, roasted nuts and tobacco, with a syrupy body. Italian espresso blends have always used 10–30 % Robusta for its thick crema and its kick.

Climate change is also making the question practical. Studies of Arabica's suitable land expect it to shrink sharply by 2050 as mountains warm. Hybrids such as Catimor and Sarchimor already carry Robusta genes for rust resistance, and wild species such as *C. stenophylla*, which tolerates heat yet tastes like Arabica, are being planted again.

## The varieties you will meet in this course

- **Typica**: the original Yemen line, elegant and clean, low yield.
- **Bourbon**: a mutation from Réunion island, sweeter, red or yellow cherries.
- **Caturra**: a dwarf Bourbon from Brazil, the backbone of Colombian farms like the one we follow.
- **Castillo**: Colombia's rust-resistant hybrid, bred by Cenicafé.
- **Gesha (Geisha)**: from the Gori Gesha forest in Ethiopia, floral and tea-like, famous since a Panama auction in 2004.
- **SL28 and SL34**: Kenyan selections with blackcurrant acidity.
