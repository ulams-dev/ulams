**At five in the morning** on a hillside in Huila, Colombia, 1,750 metres above the sea, a picker reaches for a cherry the colour of a ripe plum. Six weeks and nine thousand kilometres later, its seed ends up in your grinder. This course follows that one cherry all the way, and teaches you what happens at every step and why it changes what you taste.

*The Coffee Atlas* is a slow course. There are no streaks, no leaderboards and no timers you have to beat. There are six chapters you read, watch and listen to at your own pace, a roast simulator, a guided cupping session, and at the end a recipe that is yours.

## What you will learn

- Where coffee grows and why altitude, variety and species shape the cup
- How washed, natural, honey and anaerobic processing work, and how to recognise them by taste
- How to read a roast curve: turning point, first crack, development time
- The maths of brewing: ratios, TDS and extraction yield, and how to fix a sour or bitter cup
- How to cup like a Q-grader and describe coffee with the SCA flavour wheel
- How to design, test and write down your own signature recipe

## How it works

| Chapter | You will |
|---|---|
| I. Origins | watch the harvest, explore the coffee belt, compare Arabica and Robusta |
| II. The farm | hear from a producer, dissect a cherry, learn the processing methods |
| III. Roasting | read a roast curve, roast a virtual batch, start your roaster's logbook |
| IV. Brewing | master ratios and extraction, follow a pour-over masterclass |
| V. Tasting | learn the flavour wheel, cup at home, take the tasting check |
| VI. Your signature cup | brew three variations and send your recipe card to the tutors |

## What you need

A scale that reads 0.1 g, a burr grinder (or a roaster who grinds to order), a kettle, a pour-over dripper such as a V60, and two bags of freshly roasted coffee. About nine hours spread over a few weeks.
