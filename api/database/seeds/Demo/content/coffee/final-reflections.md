## Glossary

**Bloom** — the first small pour that wets the grounds and releases CO₂; the bed swells and bubbles.
**Body** — the weight and texture of coffee on the tongue, from tea-like to syrupy.
**Charge** — the moment green beans drop into the hot roaster; also the drum temperature at that moment.
**Cupping** — the standard way to taste coffee: fixed ratio, coarse grind, no filter, slurped from a spoon.
**Development time** — the part of the roast after first crack.
**Extraction yield (EY)** — the share of the dry coffee dose dissolved into the beverage.
**First crack** — the audible popping at ~196 °C when steam and CO₂ split the bean.
**Green coffee** — the dried, hulled, unroasted seed; the form coffee is traded in.
**Mucilage** — the sticky, sugary layer between pulp and parchment.
**Parchment** — the papery hull around the seed, removed before export.
**Peaberry** — a single round seed in a cherry instead of the usual two flat ones.
**Q-grader** — a taster certified by the Coffee Quality Institute to score coffee to SCA standards.
**Rate of rise (RoR)** — how fast the bean temperature climbs, in °C per minute.
**Specialty coffee** — coffee scoring 80 points or more on the SCA cupping form.
**TDS** — total dissolved solids: the strength of the brew.
**Terroir** — everything about a place (soil, altitude, climate) that shows up in the cup.
