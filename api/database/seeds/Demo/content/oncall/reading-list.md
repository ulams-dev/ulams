# Reading list

Optional, but these are the texts the cohort keeps coming back to.

## Books

| Title | Authors | Read it for |
|---|---|---|
| *Site Reliability Engineering* | Beyer, Jones, Petoff, Murphy (Google) | chapters 12–15: troubleshooting, emergency response, managing incidents, postmortem culture |
| *The Site Reliability Workbook* | Beyer, Murphy, Rensin, Kawahara, Thorne | chapter 5 "Alerting on SLOs" and chapter 9 "Incident Response" |
| *Implementing Service Level Objectives* | Alex Hidalgo | choosing SLIs that match what users feel |
| *Seeking SRE* | David N. Blank-Edelman (ed.) | essays from many companies, many disagreements |
| *The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'* | Sidney Dekker | why blame makes systems less safe |
| *Release It!* (2nd ed.) | Michael T. Nygard | stability patterns: timeouts, bulkheads, circuit breakers |

## Papers and articles

- Richard I. Cook, **"How Complex Systems Fail"** (1998): eighteen short points; read it before every postmortem.
- John Allspaw, **"Blameless PostMortems and a Just Culture"**, Etsy Code as Craft (2012).
- Lisanne Bainbridge, **"Ironies of Automation"**, *Automatica* (1983): why automation makes the remaining human work harder.
- David D. Woods et al., **"Behind Human Error"** (2nd ed., 2010), chapter 1.
- The **Learning from Incidents** community (learningfromincidents.io) and the **VOID** database of public incident reports.

## Talks

- **"Less Alarming Alerts!"**, SREcon16, on cutting alert noise (the talk embedded in module 1).
- Charity Majors, talks on observability-driven development and on-call that is "not a punishment".

## Practise

Pick one public postmortem from the VOID each week. Read it with the template from module 4 next to you and look for the contributing factors the authors did not list.
