# How big is space? 🌌

Space is so big that kilometres stop being useful. Astronomers measure it with **light** instead.

![How far light travels]({{asset:space-scale.png}})

## The fastest thing there is

Light travels **300,000 km every second**. That is fast enough to go around the Earth **7½ times** in one second!

$$
40\,000\ \text{km (once around Earth)} \times 7.5 = 300\,000\ \text{km}
$$

## Let's do some space maths

**How long does moonlight take to reach us?** The Moon is about 384,000 km away.

$$
\frac{384\,000\ \text{km}}{300\,000\ \text{km per second}} \approx 1.3\ \text{seconds}
$$

So when you look at the Moon, you see it as it was **1.3 seconds ago**.

**And sunlight?** The Sun is about 150,000,000 km away.

$$
\frac{150\,000\,000\ \text{km}}{300\,000\ \text{km per second}} = 500\ \text{seconds} \approx 8\ \text{minutes}
$$

If the Sun switched off (don't worry, it won't!), we would not know for 8 minutes.

## Light-years

A **light-year** is how far light travels in one year: about **9.5 million million km**. It is a distance, not a time!

| Object | Distance | Light takes… |
|---|---|---|
| Moon | 384,000 km | 1.3 seconds |
| Sun | 150 million km | 8 minutes |
| Neptune | 4.5 billion km | 4 hours |
| Proxima Centauri (nearest star) | 40 million million km | 4.2 years |
| Centre of our Milky Way | — | 26,000 years |
| Andromeda galaxy | — | 2.5 million years |

> 💡 **Fun fact:** On a dark night you can see the Andromeda galaxy with your own eyes, as a faint smudge near Cassiopeia. The light entering your eye left Andromeda **2.5 million years ago**, when the first humans were just starting to make stone tools.

## Our galaxy, the Milky Way

The Sun is one of about **100–400 billion stars** in the Milky Way. Our galaxy is a giant spinning spiral, and we live in one of its arms. And the Milky Way is just one of **trillions** of galaxies in the universe.

## Try it yourself

1. Sound travels about 340 metres every second. How many times faster is light? *(about 880,000 times!)*
2. A spaceship flies at 40,000 km per hour. How many hours to reach the Moon? *(about 9.6 hours)*
