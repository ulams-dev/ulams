::: richtext title="8.1 Other ideas that carry his name" duration="12 min" intro="Borsuk–Ulam, Hyers–Ulam, Orion, and where he worked later."
From 1965 Ulam chaired mathematics at the University of Colorado in Boulder. He was also Graduate Research Professor at the University of Florida from 1974 to 1984, and at his death he was professor of biomathematics at Colorado {{src:U-01,U-05,U-49}}. His books include *A Collection of Mathematical Problems* (1960) and the memoir *Adventures of a Mathematician* (1976) {{src:U-01}}.

**The Borsuk–Ulam theorem.** In plain words: any continuous map from a sphere to the plane sends some pair of opposite points to the same point. Karol Borsuk proved it in a 1933 paper, and a footnote of that paper says "Dieser Satz wurde als Vermutung von St. Ulam aufgestellt": the theorem was stated as a conjecture by Ulam {{src:U-36}}.

**Hyers–Ulam stability.** A stability property of functional equations is named after Hyers and Ulam. D. H. Hyers published "On the Stability of the Linear Functional Equation" in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* in 1941 {{src:U-25}}.

**Project Orion.** Ulam, with C. J. Everett, proposed the "Orion" plan. Their Los Alamos report LAMS-1955, "On a method of propulsion of projectiles by means of external nuclear explosions", is dated August 1955 {{src:U-01,U-48}}.

**Problem 38.** Ulam's Problem 38 of the Scottish Book, about chains of friends, is seen today as a forerunner of random graph theory {{src:U-33}}.

Ulam died on 13 May 1984 in Santa Fe, New Mexico {{src:U-01,U-04,U-05}}. His papers are held by the American Philosophical Society, Mss.Ms.Coll.54 {{src:U-06}}.
:::

::: richtext title="8.2 Why ulams" duration="5 min" intro="Why the product is named after Ulam, and what the name does and does not claim."
ULAMS is named in tribute to the mathematician Stanisław Ulam (1909–1984). The project is not affiliated with or endorsed by his estate, Los Alamos National Laboratory or any institution associated with him.

**What follows is the product's own view, not history.** The Scottish Book was a shared record of open questions, and each question was tied to the person who asked it {{src:U-12}}. ULAMS tries to keep courses tied to their sources in the same spirit, so that every statement of a course can be traced to where it came from. The name makes no claim about what Ulam thought of education or software.
:::
