::: richtext title="1.1 A boy from Lemberg" duration="8 min" intro="Where and when Stanisław Ulam was born, and who his family was."
Stanisław Ulam was born on 13 April 1909 in Lemberg (in Polish, Lwów), then in the Austrian province of Galicia. Today it is Lviv, in Ukraine {{src:U-01,U-05,U-29}}. The course writes Lwów for the city of Ulam's time and Lviv for the city today.

His father was Józef Ulam (1879 to 1941), a lawyer, and his mother Hania Auerbach (1887 to 1938), known as Anna. He had a sister, Stefania, born in 1912, and a brother, Adam, born on 8 April 1922 {{src:U-01}}.

::: richtext title="1.2 Kuratowski's student" duration="9 min" intro="How a first-year engineering student became a mathematician."
Ulam entered the Lwów Polytechnic in 1927 to study electrical engineering. In his first year he took Kazimierz Kuratowski's set theory course, and his first two papers, in 1929, solved problems that Kuratowski had posed in his lectures {{src:U-01}}. In 1930 he strengthened a 1929 result of Stefan Banach in measure theory by proving it without the Generalised Continuum Hypothesis {{src:U-01}}.

He took his doctorate in 1933 at the Lwów Polytechnic, with a thesis written under Kuratowski. Later Ulam claimed that he wrote the thesis in six hours during a single night; MacTutor reports this as his own claim {{src:U-01}}. The Mathematics Genealogy Project lists Kuratowski as his advisor and Włodzimierz Stożek as a second one {{src:U-28}}. The biography page of the Los Alamos Center for Nonlinear Studies says that he studied under Banach {{src:U-04}}, but it follows an older version of the MacTutor text, and the sources above name Kuratowski. Banach was a close colleague, not the supervisor.

In 1935 John von Neumann invited Ulam to the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton {{src:U-01}}. The next module follows him there.
:::
