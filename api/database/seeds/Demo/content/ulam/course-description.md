**Open the Scottish Book.** This free course follows Stanisław Ulam and the mathematicians of the Lwów School from the Polish city of Lwów (today Lviv) to Los Alamos, through the problems they set each other, and lets you try the ideas yourself in live interactives before you read the explanation.

## How the course works

- **Play.** Each lesson starts from an interactive you can work with: a map of the journey, a set of notebook cards, a spiral of primes, a random experiment, a grid that grows.
- **Read.** A short story and explanation say what you found and where it came from. Every statement is checked against a published source and cited, and where careful sources disagree the lesson says so.
- **Check yourself.** Short quizzes tell you straight away what you got right.

## What is in it

Eight modules: Lwów, the Lwów School and the Scottish Café, America and the war, Monte Carlo, the Teller–Ulam design (briefly), the prime spiral and cellular automata, Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou, and Ulam's legacy and why this product is called ulams. A final test of 14 questions, a certificate, and a last lesson that lists every source and credits every photograph.

The course is free, open to everyone and takes about three and a half hours. The text is licensed CC BY 4.0.
