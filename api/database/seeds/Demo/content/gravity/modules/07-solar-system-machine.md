Light takes 499 seconds, 8 minutes 19 seconds, to reach the Earth from the Sun {{src:G-14}}. The solar wind blows at about 400 km/s {{src:G-36}} and the Earth's magnetic field carves a cavity in it {{src:G-35}}. Around every body is a sphere of influence inside which its pull dominates.

::: interactive title="Five parking spots" start=lagrange end=lagrange duration="7 min"
Space has five spots, the Lagrange points, where the Sun's and the Earth's gravity and the turning of the frame balance. L1 and L2 are about 1.5 million km from the Earth {{src:G-22}}; SOHO and DSCOVR watch the Sun from L1, and Webb and Euclid look away from it from L2 {{src:G-24}}.

**Try this.** Follow the camera to each point. Which two are hilltops that trap objects and which three are saddles that shed them?
:::

::: interactive title="Resonance" start=resonance end=resonance duration="4 min"
When orbital periods line up in small whole numbers, repeated tugs add up. Io, Europa and Ganymede are locked in a 4 : 2 : 1 resonance: for every orbit of Ganymede, Europa makes two and Io four {{src:G-54}}.

**Try this.** Watch the three moons return to the same alignment. Count how many orbits Io makes before they line up again.
:::

::: richtext title="Machinery of the system" duration="12 min"
**Eight minutes nineteen.** 1 AU is 149,597,870,700 m and light travels 299,792,458 m/s, so the light of the Sun takes 499 s to reach the Earth {{src:G-14}}. Light from Neptune's distance (30.07 AU) takes about 4.2 hours. Gravity moves at the same speed, so if the Sun vanished the Earth would keep curving along its orbit for another 8 minutes 19 seconds.

**A magnetic shield.** The Sun blows a wind of charged particles outward at about 400 km/s, about a million tonnes a second {{src:G-36}}. The Earth's field, generated by the churning metal of its core, deflects it, squashing the field to some six to ten Earth radii on the sunward side and stretching it into a tail past the Moon behind {{src:G-35}}.

**Spheres of influence.** Near a body its gravity dominates, so the Moon orbits the Earth and not the Sun directly. The radius of the Earth's sphere is about a ≈ 0.92 million km (our calculation from the Earth-Sun mass ratio, a·(m/M)^(2/5) {{src:G-03}}), and the Moon, at 384,400 km, lies inside it.

**Lagrange points.** In the frame that turns with the Earth, the two gravities and the outward push of the turning balance at five places. L1, L2 and L3 lie on the Sun-Earth line and are unstable saddles: a probe drifts off unless it fires its thrusters from time to time. L4 and L5 lie 60° ahead of and behind the Earth on its orbit and are stable: they trap objects, which is why Trojan asteroids gather there. Two Earth Trojans are known, 2010 TK7 and 2020 XL5 {{src:G-53}} {{src:G-26}}. ESA's Vigil mission is planned for L5, with launch in 2031 {{src:G-25}}.

**Which point for which job?** L1 sits 1.5 million km toward the Sun and always sees it: SOHO and DSCOVR watch the Sun and the Earth from there. L2 lies 1.5 million km on the far side and always faces away from both: Webb, Euclid, WMAP and Planck work there {{src:G-22}} {{src:G-23}}. L3, behind the Sun, can never be seen from the Earth.

**Resonance.** In Jupiter's system the periods of Io, Europa and Ganymede are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 4 {{src:G-54}}. Venus and the Earth are close to 13 : 8 and draw a five-petalled rose over eight years {{src:G-06}}.
:::
