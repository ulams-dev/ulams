# Remote Work and Data Handling Policy

This policy sets out how employees work away from the office and how company and customer data must be handled
wherever the work happens. It applies to every employee and contractor, including people on fixed-term contracts. It is an original policy written for
demonstration and tests; it is not legal advice.

## Working remotely

### Approval and working hours

An employee may work remotely for up to three days a week after the line manager has approved the arrangement in
the HR system. The approval names the days and the place of work. Core hours are 10:00 to 15:00 in the employee's
local time zone; the employee must be reachable on the team chat during core hours. A change of place of work that
leaves the country needs separate approval from HR because tax, insurance and data rules differ between countries.

### Equipment and workspace

The company provides a laptop, a headset and, on request, a second monitor. Company equipment is used for company
work only and is never lent to family members. The workspace must allow the screen to be unobserved: no work with
customer data in cafes, trains or other public places where others can read the screen. Lost or stolen equipment is
reported to the service desk within two hours of the loss being noticed.

## Handling data

### Classifying data

Data is public, internal, confidential or restricted. Customer personal data and unpublished financial results are
restricted. Restricted data is stored only in the approved document system and the customer relationship system, never
on local disks, personal e-mail, personal cloud storage or messaging apps. Internal data may be shared with colleagues
through the company chat and e-mail, and with external parties only after the data owner agrees.

### Sharing and retention

Before sharing a file outside the company, the employee checks the recipient's address, removes hidden sheets and
comments, and uses a link that expires within thirty days. Customer data is kept only as long as the contract or the
law requires. Exports of customer data for analysis are anonymised before they leave the system that holds them.

## Security basics

### Accounts and devices

Every account uses multi-factor authentication. Passwords are unique and kept in the company password manager.
Devices lock after five minutes without activity and receive updates within seven days of their release. Employees
connect to company systems through the company VPN when they use a network they do not control.

### Reporting incidents

A suspected data breach, a phishing e-mail that was opened or any lost device is reported to the security team at once
through the incident form or by phone. Reporting in good faith never leads to disciplinary action; hiding an incident
may. The security team decides whether customers and the regulator must be told, within the deadlines the law sets.
